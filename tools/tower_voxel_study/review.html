Prototype and validation notes

This is a silent, isolated Godot visual study using existing bee and tower artwork. The field is a synthetic fixture, not a running match. No production game files, combat rules, hit timing, or simulation state have changed. Live controls: Space pauses, S toggles quarter speed, R restarts, arrow keys step, Escape closes.

Thirty texture-sampled chunks preserve the bee silhouette at the first impact sample. Instanced cube geometry has shaded faces and local contact shadows. Flight, bounce, tumble, slide and fade are sampled from explicit event times; the shaders do not use a global clock. The longer skid lasts 1.15–1.75 seconds after the final bounce, with constant friction bringing each piece to an exact stop. Approximately one in ten heavy fragments keeps a much smaller impulse. The crowding rule preserves the full bounce, slide and minimum resting interval before fading. A fixed capacity admits complete breakups only; extreme overload skips excess visual events. Apparent shallow piling uses a visual height offset, not chunk-to-chunk rigid-body collisions.

Checked: silhouette alignment, shot direction, bounce continuity, time-sampling invariance at 30/60/120 Hz, density-dependent decay, bounds, fixed node counts, seeking/replay and full cleanup. All 780 final chunk positions were checked against both lane corridors, including cube extent: 696 clear both lanes, with a median 43.2-pixel ground slide. This is a measurement of the synthetic fixture. Captured with Godot 4.2 Compatibility on this Mac; phone performance and production integration have not been evaluated. The 60 fps video is an offline capture, not a device performance result.
