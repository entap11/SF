
# Swarmfront Marketing & Launch Rollout
## Production Plan and Execution Standard

**Status:** Active production plan  
**Planning target:** December 1, 2026 launch  
**Public launch date:** Not yet committed  
**Initial channels:** Website, Reddit, YouTube, Discord  
**Campaign model:** Weekly single-argument manifesto releases building to a launch crescendo  
**Primary product:** Swarmfront  
**Studio:** ENTaP

---

# 1. Purpose

This document is the controlling production plan for the first public Swarmfront marketing campaign and launch runway.

The campaign has two jobs:

1. **Show that Swarmfront is a game worth playing.**
2. **Show, through specific observable commitments, that ENTaP is building mobile games differently on purpose.**

The campaign must not ask players to trust vague claims such as “player-first,” “fair,” or “different.”

The governing standard is:

> **Make specific promises and show the receipts.**

Every public philosophical claim should eventually connect to a visible product decision, development practice, beta response, or gameplay rule.

---

# 2. Core campaign thesis

Swarmfront is a fast competitive strategy game built for people who love mobile games and are tired of what mobile games have become.

The broader ENTaP thesis is:

> **The player is the customer, not a resource to be harvested.**

The campaign should consistently distinguish between:

- Making a good business.
- Building manipulative monetization.
- Creating useful progression.
- Manufacturing friction and selling its removal.
- Building intelligent opponents.
- Secretly manipulating difficulty for retention.
- Making a cinematic advertisement.
- Advertising gameplay that does not exist.

The campaign is not “mobile games are bad.”

It is:

> **Many common mobile-game practices are bad for players, unnecessary for good game design, and not required to build a viable business. We are attempting to prove another way.**

---

# 3. Public-claim standard

Every manifesto principle must pass this test:

> **Could a player catch us violating it?**

Preferred claims are observable and falsifiable.

Examples:

- You cannot buy XP.
- We do not secretly make bots easier because you lose or harder because you win.
- The game shown in the ad is the game you play.
- A returning player can reach a normal match in approximately 1–3 intentional actions.
- One store/spend currency is enough.
- Progression, rank, and store currency do not morph into one another.
- We do not create artificial gameplay friction and then sell its removal.
- Competitive advantage is not sold.
- Competition is the core product, not a late-added side mode.

Avoid unsupported marketing abstractions such as:

- “We respect players.”
- “We care about quality.”
- “Fair gaming.”
- “Built by gamers for gamers.”

Those may be conclusions a player reaches. They are not sufficient evidence.

---

# 4. Claim-status discipline

Every public principle must be classified internally as one of:

- **SHIPPED** — true in the currently available build.
- **V1 COMMITMENT** — approved and required for launch, but not yet publicly claimable as shipped.
- **FUTURE PRINCIPLE** — studio doctrine or roadmap intent that must not be presented as a current product feature.

Marketing copy must never silently promote a V1 commitment or future principle into a shipped claim.

When the status is uncertain, do not publish the claim until product ownership confirms it.

---

# 5. Bot and difficulty doctrine

Swarmfront may use bots that:

- Adapt tactically during a match.
- React to the player's strategy.
- Detect and abandon unproductive tactics.
- Express distinct personalities or strategic styles.
- Become more human-like in how they respond to battlefield conditions.

Swarmfront must not market those systems as “bots never adapt.”

The accurate doctrine is:

> **The difficulty you choose is the difficulty you get. We do not secretly make the game easier because you lost or harder because you won. But the bots can adapt tactically during the battle, because good opponents do.**

Approved conceptual distinction:

> **Bots adapt to the battle, not to your retention profile.**

Do not imply:

- Fixed scripts when bots are adaptive.
- Hidden result-based rubber-banding.
- Loss-streak mercy.
- Win-streak punishment.
- Store-triggered difficulty.
- Paywalls disguised as difficulty progression.

---

# 6. Currency doctrine

Public messaging should distinguish between functional systems rather than describing every tracked value as interchangeable “currency.”

The current conceptual rule is:

- One clear store/spend currency.
- Progression measures remain progression measures.
- Competitive ranking/stake systems remain competitive systems.
- One type does not silently convert into another.
- No multi-stage conversion chain exists to obscure real value.

Approved argument:

> **One spendable currency is enough.**

Supporting doctrine:

> **No currency alchemy. Progress, rank, and store value do not pass through conversion chains designed to make players lose track of what something costs.**

Any public explanation must match the final V1 economy exactly.

---

# 7. Advertising truth doctrine

The campaign may use:

- Cinematic presentation.
- Blender-rendered storytelling.
- Dramatic editing.
- Music.
- Stylized camera work.
- Exaggerated emotional framing.

It may not depict a fundamentally different game.

Governing rule:

> **Our spots may make Swarmfront look epic. They may never make Swarmfront look like a game that does not exist.**

Public shorthand:

> **The game in the ad is the game you play.**

Gameplay claims made inside a cinematic must remain true of the actual product.

---

# 8. Campaign architecture

The campaign is a sustained runway, not a launch-day content dump.

The desired arc is:

**Shared grievance → specific ENTaP rule → Swarmfront proof → broader studio philosophy → launch**

The manifesto is not released all at once.

Instead, approximately one major argument is released per week over roughly 2–3 months, with the strongest pieces sequenced toward launch.

The weekly format should create ongoing conversation rather than ask the audience to consume a 50-point corporate doctrine in one sitting.

The campaign should gradually move from:

> “Yes, that mobile-game practice sucks.”

to:

> “Who is building this differently?”

to:

> “I want to try Swarmfront.”

---

# 9. Content waves

## Wave A — Universal mobile-game grievances

Lead with topics that are understandable without prior knowledge of Swarmfront.

Examples:

- The game in the ad should be the game.
- Why does it take nine popups to start playing?
- You cannot buy XP from us.
- One spendable currency is enough.
- We do not sell the solution to friction we created.
- We do not manufacture impossible progression walls and sell the shortcut through them.

Primary goal:

> Create conversation around widely recognized player frustrations.

Swarmfront appears as evidence, not as the entire argument.

## Wave B — Competitive integrity

Once the audience understands the studio's point of view, move deeper into competitive doctrine.

Examples:

- Competition is the point, not the afterthought.
- What “no pay-to-win” actually means.
- The difficulty you choose is the difficulty you get.
- Bots adapt to the battle, not to your retention profile.
- Progress must be earned.
- Competitive skill should remain meaningful when account advantages are removed.

Primary goal:

> Establish Swarmfront as a competitive game with deliberate integrity rules rather than a progression economy with PvP attached.

## Wave C — Studio philosophy and proof

Closer to launch, connect the individual arguments into the broader ENTaP position.

Examples:

- Why the player is the customer.
- Why ENTaP publishes beta criticism.
- Why listening to players does not mean blindly implementing their proposed fix.
- How an actual beta complaint changed the game.
- The 50 observable ways ENTaP is building differently.
- Why evidence and fail-closed release gates matter.
- What the studio believes mobile games could become.

Primary goal:

> Convert a series of opinions into a coherent studio identity immediately before product availability.

# 10. Beta-feedback content series

Create a recurring editorial format:

## FROM THE BETA

Each selected post uses this structure:

### What the tester told us

Preserve the substance of the tester's actual criticism.

Do not selectively rewrite criticism into praise.

Obtain appropriate permission before publishing personally identifying information or direct attribution.

### What we think was underneath it

Explain the design problem ENTaP believes the tester actually experienced.

### What we changed

Show the fix, prototype, rebalance, visual change, new test, bot behavior, map revision, or UX revision.

### Or: why we did not use their solution

When appropriate, explain:

- Why the tester's observation was valid.
- Why the proposed fix would create another problem.
- What alternative response was chosen.

This series should demonstrate:

> Listening to players means taking their experience seriously enough to understand the cause, not automatically implementing every requested feature.

Candidate early example:

A tester requested a 1.5× or 2× speed control because a match became boring.

Possible response story:

- The boredom was valid.
- Fast-forward would hide the problem.
- The map had a known stalemate issue.
- Bots should recognize ineffective equilibrium and change tactics.
- Unit speed itself may also have been slowed too far.
- ENTaP chose to fix underlying gameplay instead of adding a button to accelerate the bad state.

# 11. Channel scope

Initial campaign channels are deliberately narrow.

## Website

Canonical home for Swarmfront, manifesto principles, Dispatches/blogettes, beta-response pieces, video, community links, and release/beta CTA.

## Reddit

Primary discussion channel.

Doctrine:

> **Start conversations. Do not distribute press releases.**

Every post must fit the community, follow subreddit self-promotion rules, disclose developer affiliation when relevant, avoid copy-pasting identical promotional text across communities, and remain active in the discussion after posting.

## YouTube

Primary video home for hero spots, alternate cinematic spots, gameplay, development pieces, longer design explanations when justified, and Shorts when production overhead is low.

## Discord

Community home once there is enough external interest to support a real community.

Do not create an empty Discord merely to have another icon in the website footer.

## Deferred channels

Do not prioritize TikTok, Instagram, X, Threads, Facebook, or other high-maintenance social networks.

Add those when audience scale and staffing justify a dedicated marketing/distribution role.

# 12. Website V0

The initial website should be intentionally small.

Do not build a platform.

Required destinations:

## Home

Must answer within approximately ten seconds:

- What is Swarmfront?
- Why should I care?
- What can I do next?

Recommended structure:

1. Swarmfront hero.
2. Actual gameplay or approved hero spot.
3. One-sentence game pitch.
4. “Built differently. On purpose.”
5. Four to six strongest observable principles.
6. Gameplay proof.
7. Beta/follow/community CTA.

## Game

Include core gameplay explanation, actual gameplay video, modes, screenshots, competitive premise, and beta/release availability.

Lead with the game. The philosophy supports the product; it does not replace the need for Swarmfront to look fun.

## Manifesto

Canonical index for the studio principles.

Organize by categories such as Time, Money, Competition, Progression, Honesty, UX, and Development.

Do not initially force readers through 50 uninterrupted paragraphs.

Each principle may link to a focused Dispatch.

## Dispatches

Houses manifesto blogettes, FROM THE BETA pieces, and selected development/design essays.

Prefer short, focused arguments.

Target range: approximately 300–700 words for ordinary manifesto pieces unless a topic genuinely requires more.

## Community

Links to Reddit, YouTube, and Discord.

May include beta CTA if appropriate.

## About / Contact

Keep minimal.

Include why ENTaP exists, basic studio identity, and a press/contact method.

Do not build an elaborate corporate biography.

# 13. Website engineering standard

The website should be built using the simplest proven stack that satisfies the need.

Mandatory ENTaP reuse-first gate:

Before building new testing, deployment, analytics, form, hosting, content, or engineering plumbing:

1. Search the current repository.
2. Search proven ENTaP projects.
3. Search shared tooling.
4. Search historical tooling.
5. Search engine/platform-native tooling.
6. Prefer use, configure, extend, extract, or adapt.
7. Build a new parallel system only when reuse is unsuitable and the reason is documented.

V0 should not introduce an unnecessary application backend, custom CMS without need, new authentication system, custom analytics platform, bespoke media pipeline, elaborate animation framework, 3D interactive homepage, or infrastructure justified only by hypothetical future needs.

Preferred capabilities:

- Static or mostly static rendering.
- Markdown/content-driven manifesto and Dispatch posts.
- Responsive layout.
- Fast page loads.
- Video embedding.
- SEO metadata.
- Social sharing metadata.
- Sitemap.
- Privacy-conscious basic analytics.
- Simple beta/contact CTA.
- Automated or one-command deployment.
- Mobile-first validation.

---

# 14. Website visual doctrine

The website should feel related to Swarmfront without becoming an in-game menu.

Use existing Swarmfront/ENTaP visual language, strong imagery, dark surfaces where appropriate, restraint, real gameplay, and current approved logos/renders.

Avoid overbuilding the page into a game UI, illegible futuristic body typography, constant glow, huge motion sequences before content appears, scroll-jacking, long intro animations, fake game interfaces, and decorative complexity that hurts mobile load or reading.

Primary objective:

> Make the game and argument easy to understand.

---

# 15. Marketing visual-production track

Swarmfront already has a Blender-based hero-spot system in development.

The objective is not to independently create 15 unrelated advertisements.

The objective is:

> Perfect one production-quality cinematic asset/render pipeline that can generate several meaningfully different spots efficiently.

Known work remains before certification, including production-render cleanup such as preventing bee skeleton/internal rig artifacts from appearing in rendered art.

Do not publish final spots until the hero visual pipeline is clean.

Target eventual spot family:

- Hero spot.
- Competition spot.
- Swarm spectacle spot.
- Manifesto-adjacent spot.
- Launch spot.

Three to five genuinely distinct spots are preferable to a large volume of minimally changed versions.

---

# 16. Marketing capture candidate

After the remaining major gameplay/visual modification is complete, create a dedicated Marketing Capture Candidate.

Record exact Git SHA, build version, platform, device, resolution, maps, team colors, match fixtures, bot settings, graphics settings, and any approved staged setup.

Capture only from a build that has passed a visual-marketing audit.

Minimum audit:

- No debug overlays.
- No placeholder copy.
- No obsolete sprites.
- No known high-visibility rendering defects.
- Current approved hive visuals.
- Current approved bee visuals.
- Current approved lane presentation.
- Current approved shadows.
- Current approved unit speed.
- Current approved camera/projection.
- Current approved VFX.

Store clean raw gameplay capture separately from edited marketing output.

Never lose provenance for footage used publicly.

# 17. Website quiet launch

Before public promotion:

1. Deploy the site.
2. Test every page on representative phone, tablet, and desktop classes.
3. Verify video playback and fallbacks.
4. Verify social-card previews.
5. Verify SEO metadata.
6. Verify sitemap.
7. Verify community links.
8. Verify beta/contact CTA.
9. Verify analytics.
10. Verify performance.
11. Have unfamiliar users identify what Swarmfront is and what they can do next.

The site should exist quietly before the first major traffic spike.

# 18. Weekly publication cadence

Target approximately one primary manifesto/Dispatch argument per week.

Supporting content may include a related gameplay clip, YouTube cut, FROM THE BETA entry, development screenshot, Discord discussion, or follow-up Reddit discussion.

Do not force daily posting.

No artificial content quota should reduce game-development quality.

The weekly argument is the campaign spine.

---

# 19. Launch crescendo

The campaign should increase product specificity as launch approaches.

## Early runway

Primary emphasis: shared grievances, philosophy, and conversation. Swarmfront appears as proof.

## Middle runway

Increase gameplay, beta-response stories, development evidence, hero-spot presence, and competitive explanations.

## Final month

Increase strongest manifesto arguments, gameplay proof, cinematic spots, beta outcomes, the 50 Ways compilation, release-window messaging, and community calls to action.

## Final week

The philosophy and product should converge.

Campaign destination:

> **We've spent months telling you what we think mobile games should be. Now you can play ours.**

Exact launch copy requires final creative approval.

December 1, 2026 is the current internal planning target.

Work backward from that date for website readiness, weekly manifesto cadence, cinematic completion, marketing capture, beta-response content, store readiness, community setup, and final product certification.

# 22. Beta-content ethics

Before publicly using beta feedback:

- Remove or obtain permission for personally identifying information.
- Do not misquote.
- Do not imply praise where the tester criticized.
- Do not manufacture composite “testimonials.”
- Distinguish verbatim quotes from paraphrase.
- Do not embarrass a tester for a misunderstanding.
- Do not expose private beta information they reasonably expected to remain private.
- Focus criticism on the product and the studio's response.

The goal is transparency, not using testers as marketing props.

---

# 23. Success criteria before public campaign start

Do not begin the sustained campaign until:

- Marketing Canon v1 is approved.
- Strongest public claims have been checked against product reality.
- Website V0 is usable.
- At least several weeks of manifesto content are publication-ready.
- Hero visual pipeline is credible enough that product footage will not undermine the argument.
- Community destinations are functional.
- There is a clear CTA.
- Swarmfront is visually stable enough that early public material will not immediately look obsolete.
- The team can respond to inbound beta/community interest.

The entire game does not need to be final.

The public-facing story must be true.

# 24. Production gates

## Gate M0 — Marketing canon

Pass when the core pitch, studio thesis, claim-status model, bot language, currency language, advertising-truth rule, and initial 50 principles are approved.

## Gate M1 — Website skeleton

Pass when the repository/stack decision is documented; Home/Game/Manifesto/Dispatches/Community/About routes exist; the responsive shell passes mobile/desktop smoke; the content system works; and no unnecessary backend has been introduced.

## Gate M2 — Content bank

Pass when the existing 12–15 arguments are inventoried; each has status, category, publication readiness, and proof needs; the initial weekly order is proposed; and at least four consecutive posts are publication-ready.

## Gate M3 — Hero pipeline

Pass when Blender/render defects are resolved; bee art does not expose rig/skeleton artifacts; the hero spot is approved; and the adaptation path to additional spots is proven.

## Gate M4 — Marketing capture candidate

Pass when the major intended visual/gameplay change is complete; exact capture SHA is recorded; capture audit passes; and clean gameplay footage exists.

## Gate M5 — Quiet website

Pass when the production site is deployed; device/browser QA passes; social/SEO metadata passes; CTA passes; analytics passes; and no broken/placeholder destination remains.

## Gate M6 — Campaign start

Pass when the first publication sequence is locked; Reddit/YouTube/Discord destinations are ready; community-response capacity exists; and public claims match current product truth.

## Gate M7 — Launch crescendo

Pass when the launch candidate is credible; store/release path is ready; strongest content assets are complete; and release timing is approved for public announcement.

# 26. Governance

All marketing implementation follows ENTaP reuse-first governance.

All product claims must remain subordinate to the actual product.

Marketing may dramatize.

Marketing may simplify.

Marketing may provoke.

Marketing may not lie.

The website is the canonical home of the campaign, not a new source of gameplay truth.

Game rules remain owned by game specifications and authoritative implementation.

Marketing Canon owns public claim language.

The content tracker owns publication state.

The release process owns whether a product claim is actually shipped.

A repeated discrepancy between marketing and product is a process defect and must be corrected at the source.
